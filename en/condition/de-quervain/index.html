De Quervain's Tenosynovitis (thumb-side wrist)

The tendons that move the thumb and their tunnel become inflamed, so the thumb-side wrist hurts when lifting a baby or opening jars.

✨ Open the interactive version (symptom check · compare)

📖 What is this condition?

The two tendons that spread and extend the thumb pass through a narrow tunnel (sheath) on the thumb side of the wrist. Repeated thumb use builds friction between the tendons and the tunnel, thickening the tunnel wall so it hurts each time the tendons pass — like getting caught in a narrow door.

Lifting a baby is the classic cause, so it's also called 'mommy's wrist.' It's common in postpartum women (hormone change plus repeated lifting), heavy thumb-texters, and jobs that twist the wrist (hairdressers, cooks).

Why does it happen?

  • Lifting a baby (very common in postpartum women)
  • Thumb texting and gaming on a phone
  • Repeated thumb-plus-wrist motions (scissoring, hair styling, hand-washing)
  • Tendon swelling from pregnancy/postpartum hormone change
  • A sudden increase in hand use

✅ Do you have these symptoms?

  • Pain on the thumb-side wrist (from the thumb base toward the wrist)
  • Pain opening jar lids, turning keys, using scissors
  • A twinge in the wrist when lifting a baby
  • A catching feeling or sound in the wrist when moving the thumb
  • Slight swelling and tenderness in the painful area
  • Pain spreading toward the thumb or forearm

🔬 Self-observations you can do at home

Go slowly and stay within a pain-free range. These responses are observations, not a diagnosis.

🔍 Finkelstein test

Tuck the thumb into the palm and wrap the other four fingers over it into a fist. Then bend the wrist slowly toward the little-finger side.

For reference: Sharp pain on the thumb-side wrist is the classic sign of De Quervain's. (Even normal wrists pull a little, so compare both sides.)

🔍 Tender-spot check

Press the tendons just above the hollow that forms on the wrist when you spread the thumb (the anatomical snuffbox).

For reference: Tenderness and slight swelling when pressed makes it likely.

🔍 Telling it apart from thumb arthritis

Check whether the pain is on the thumb-side wrist tendon line or deep in the thumb-base joint.

For reference: If the thumb-base joint itself hurts and you're over 50, it may be thumb arthritis — have an X-ray to distinguish.

🤲 Hands-on care — provided by a professional

🤲 Thumb spica splint

A splint immobilizing the thumb and wrist together reduces the friction of the tendons sliding in the tunnel. A basic first-line treatment, especially when pain is severe.

🤲 Injection (by a doctor)

A steroid injection into the tendon sheath calms the swollen tunnel — fast and with a high success rate. Combined with a splint it works even better.

🤲 Manual / physical therapy

The therapist releases the stiff thumb/wrist joints and lowers forearm muscle tension. Ice (acute) or ultrasound is sometimes used to calm inflammation.

🏃 Active care — exercises you do yourself

🏃 Pain-free thumb motion

Once acute pain eases, gently draw circles with the thumb and touch the tip to each finger so the tendons don't stick.

⏱ 10 reps, 3 times a day

🏃 Isometric thumb abduction

Hold the thumb slightly spread, block it with the other hand, and push outward 5 seconds without moving.

⏱ 5 sec × 10 reps, twice a day

🏃 Eccentric wrist radial deviation (recovery phase)

Hold a hammer (or water-bottle neck) thumb-up, lift the wrist toward the thumb side, then lower it slowly toward the little-finger side over 3 seconds.

⏱ 12 reps × 3 sets, once a day

🏃 Changing how you hold a baby / phone

When lifting a baby, scoop with the whole forearm rather than bending the wrist into an 'L.' Use the index finger or both hands on a phone instead of the thumb. Without changing the trigger motion, it will surely recur.

⏱ Lifestyle — always

🚨 Stop self-care and see a doctor if…

  • Pain lasting over 3 months despite splint/injection (consider surgically opening the tunnel)
  • A severely swollen, red, hot wrist (rule out infection/gout)
  • Accompanying thumb tingling or numbness (rule out a nerve problem)
  • Pain that started after trauma and is deep on the thumb-side wrist (rule out a scaphoid fracture — needs X-ray)
