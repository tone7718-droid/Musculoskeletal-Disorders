🦒 Neck

Thoracic Outlet Syndrome (nerve/vessel squeeze to the arm)

Nerves and vessels heading to the arm are squeezed in a narrow passage below the neck and collarbone, so the arm tingles, feels heavy, and tires easily.

✨ Open the interactive version (symptom check · compare)

📖 What is this condition?

Between the side of the neck, the collarbone, and the first rib runs a narrow passage (the thoracic outlet) carrying the nerve bundle and vessels to the arm. When this passage narrows, the nerve or vessel gets squeezed — like stepping on a hose — and symptoms show up in the arm.

Most cases (over 95%) are the 'neurogenic' type from nerve compression: tingling in the inner arm and ring/little fingers, and a heavy, tingling arm when working with the arm raised (combing hair, hanging laundry, gripping a wheel). Rarely the vessel is squeezed, making the hand pale/bluish or swollen.

Forward head, rounded shoulders, carrying a heavy bag on one side, and tense neck muscles (scalenes) commonly narrow the passage. Many improve with posture correction and nerve/muscle release.

Why does it happen?

  • Forward head/rounded shoulders narrowing the space between collarbone and first rib
  • Tension/shortening of the scalenes (neck) and pectoralis minor (front chest)
  • Repeated overhead arm work/sport (hairdressing, papering, swimming, volleyball)
  • Carrying a heavy bag/backpack on one shoulder
  • A congenital cervical rib (an extra rib in the neck) or post-trauma changes

✅ Do you have these symptoms?

  • Tingling and dullness in the inner arm and the ring/little-finger side
  • The arm gets heavy and tingly when working with it raised overhead
  • Hanging laundry, combing hair, or driving tires the arm easily
  • Pressing the side of the neck/above the collarbone sends tingling down the arm
  • Carrying a bag on one side makes that arm tingle more
  • (Vascular type) the hand is cold, pale or bluish, and swells

🔬 Self-observations you can do at home

Go slowly and stay within a pain-free range. These responses are observations, not a diagnosis.

🔍 Roos test (EAST, hands-up open-close)

Hold both arms out to the side just below 'surrender,' elbows bent 90°, and slowly open and close fists for 3 minutes.

For reference: If the affected arm gets heavy and tingly first so you can't hold it, thoracic outlet syndrome is possible. (Even normal arms tire — compare both sides.)

🔍 Arm-raised posture reproduction

Hold the symptom-triggering posture (arm raised overhead) for 1 minute.

For reference: If tingling/heaviness reproduces in that posture and eases when you lower the arm, it suggests passage compression.

🔍 Distinguishing check

Note whether the tingling is in the thumb–index (median, carpal tunnel) or ring/little finger plus inner arm (thoracic outlet/ulnar).

For reference: Broad tingling up the inner arm leans toward the thoracic outlet; thumb-side tingling confined below the wrist leans toward carpal tunnel.

🤲 Hands-on care — provided by a professional

🤲 Manual therapy — scalene/pec-minor release & first-rib/collarbone mobilization

The therapist releases the scalenes (which narrow the passage) and the pectoralis minor, and adjusts the alignment of the first rib, collarbone, and shoulder to reduce nerve/vessel compression.

🤲 Neural mobilization

The pinched nerve bundle is glided smoothly through the passage by adjusting arm/neck position, reducing adhesion and hypersensitivity.

🤲 Medication / injection (by a doctor)

For severe muscle tension and pain, medication or a scalene injection may be considered. If the vascular type is suspected, vascular testing is needed.

🏃 Active care — exercises you do yourself

🏃 Posture correction — shoulders down and back

Open rounded shoulders and draw the blades down and in to widen the passage. Lowering shrugged shoulders is key.

⏱ 10 reps × 3 sets, twice a day + often

🏃 Scalene (side-neck) stretch

Gently press your own collarbone on the same side and tilt the head diagonally back to the other side to stretch the front-side of the neck. Gently only.

⏱ 20–30 sec × 3 reps (each side), twice a day

🏃 Pectoralis minor doorway stretch

Place the forearm on a doorframe slightly above shoulder height and step forward to stretch the front of the chest.

⏱ 30 sec × 3 reps (each side), twice a day

🏃 Nerve flossing (ulnar nerve glide)

Raise the arm to the side, make an 'OK' shape with the hand, and bring it toward the eye like glasses, gliding the nerve gently back and forth. Only within a range that doesn't worsen tingling.

⏱ 10 reps × 2 sets, twice a day

🏃 Lifestyle correction

Alternate the bag side or use a backpack, lighten loads, and use a step for overhead work to cut arm-raised time.

⏱ Lifestyle — always

🚨 Stop self-care and see a doctor if…

  • The hand suddenly turns pale/bluish and cold, or swells badly (vascular type — see a doctor quickly)
  • Sudden severe arm pain and swelling (possible blood clot — emergency)
  • Hand muscles wasting and weakening (nerve damage progressing)
  • Tingling that keeps worsening and wakes you at night
  • No improvement after several months of conservative care

⚠️ This material is for health information only and does not replace a doctor's diagnosis or treatment. If symptoms last more than 2 weeks or any red flag applies, see a medical professional.
