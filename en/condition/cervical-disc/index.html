🦒 Neck

Cervical Disc Herniation

A soft cushion (disc) between the neck bones bulges out and presses a nerve, so the arm and hand hurt and tingle more than the neck itself.

✨ Open the interactive version (symptom check · compare)

📖 What is this condition?

Between the neck bones (cervical spine) sit cushions called discs — like jelly donuts. The outside is a tough fibrous ring and the inside is a soft nucleus. When repeated poor posture or impact weakens the outer ring, the inner jelly pushes backward.

When the bulging disc presses a nerve root heading to the arm, oddly enough the shoulder, arm, and fingers hurt and tingle more than the neck. Like a flickering lamp when a wire is pinched, a pinched nerve shows symptoms at its far end (the arm and hand).

Good news: most neck discs improve without surgery. The bulging disc is often reabsorbed by the body over time.

Why does it happen?

  • Long-standing forward head posture concentrating pressure on the back of the disc
  • Working long hours with the head down (phone, laptop)
  • Sudden impact such as a car accident (whiplash)
  • Age-related drying and loss of disc elasticity (degeneration)

✅ Do you have these symptoms?

  • Pain or tingling running from one shoulder down the arm to the fingers
  • Arm tingling worsens when you tilt the head back or toward the painful side
  • Coughing or sneezing sends pain into the neck and arm
  • A feeling of weakness in the arm (lifting, buttoning feels clumsy)
  • Arm or inner-shoulder-blade pain that is worse than the neck pain
  • Resting the affected hand on top of your head actually eases the symptoms

🔬 Self-observations you can do at home

Go slowly and stay within a pain-free range. These responses are observations, not a diagnosis.

🔍 Hand-on-head test (shoulder abduction relief sign)

Rest the palm of the affected side on top of your head and wait 30 seconds to a minute, noting any change in arm tingling.

For reference: If the arm tingling decreases with the hand on your head, a cervical disc pinching the nerve root is likely (the nerve slackens and symptoms ease).

🔍 Tilt-and-extend (a gentle version of the Spurling test)

Seated, tilt the head back slightly and slowly toward the painful side, holding about 5 seconds. Never have anyone press down on your head.

For reference: If this reproduces tingling or pain running down the shoulder and arm, suspect nerve compression. Stop immediately if symptoms appear.

🔍 Arm strength comparison

Lift a water bottle with both hands at once, or bend both elbows and resist while someone tries to straighten them.

For reference: If the affected arm is noticeably weaker, nerve compression may be progressing — see a doctor.

🤲 Hands-on care — provided by a professional

🤲 Cervical traction

A machine or the therapist's hands gently pull the head to slightly widen the space between the neck bones. The pressure on the pinched nerve eases and arm tingling may improve.

🤲 Manual therapy — neural mobilization & joint release

The therapist gently glides the pinched nerve by adjusting and moving the arm and neck (neural mobilization) and releases the stiff upper back and surrounding muscles to lighten the neck's load.

🤲 Medication / injections (by a doctor)

In a severe acute flare with strong inflammation, anti-inflammatory drugs or a nerve-area injection may be needed to 'put out the fire.' Pain has to settle before exercise therapy can begin.

🏃 Active care — exercises you do yourself

🏃 Chin tucks

Pull the chin straight back and hold 5 seconds. This is the basic move of neck-disc rehab — it improves the direction of pressure on the disc. Reduce the range if arm tingling worsens.

⏱ 10 reps × 3 sets, 3 times a day

🏃 Nerve flossing (median nerve gliding)

Spread the affected arm out to the side, palm up. As you bend the wrist back, tilt the head toward the arm; as you release the wrist, tilt the head away. Glide the nerve gently back and forth like dental floss. Only within a range that doesn't worsen tingling.

⏱ 10 reps × 2 sets, twice a day

🏃 Upper-back extension (foam-roller thoracic extension)

Lie with the roller across your upper back and gently extend just the upper back. When the upper back opens up, the load on the neck drops.

⏱ 10 reps × 2 sets, once a day

🏃 Deep neck muscle strengthening (pre-stage head lift)

Lie without a pillow, chin slightly tucked, and lift the back of the head just 1 cm off the floor — as if lifting only 10% of its weight — holding 5 seconds. Only when you can do it without pain or tingling.

⏱ 5-second holds × 10 reps, once a day (increase hold time as it gets easier)

🏃 Shoulder-blade squeezes

Draw both shoulder blades down and in, as if tucking them into your back pockets, and hold 5 seconds. Don't let the shoulders shrug up.

⏱ 10 reps × 3 sets, twice a day

🚨 Stop self-care and see a doctor if…

  • Hand strength keeps fading, or chopsticks/buttoning get clumsier
  • Legs feel unsteady or your feet drag when walking (spinal cord compression — emergency)
  • Loss of bladder or bowel control (emergency)
  • Both arms tingle at once, or there's altered sensation in the trunk
  • Unbearable pain that doesn't budge with medication for over 2 weeks

⚠️ This material is for health information only and does not replace a doctor's diagnosis or treatment. If symptoms last more than 2 weeks or any red flag applies, see a medical professional.
