Frozen Shoulder (Adhesive Capsulitis)

The capsule wrapping the shoulder becomes inflamed and shrinks, so the shoulder stiffens in every direction.

✨ Open the interactive version (symptom check · compare)

📖 What is this condition?

The shoulder joint is wrapped in a loose bag (the capsule) that lets it move freely. In frozen shoulder this bag becomes inflamed, thickens, and shrinks — as if your clothes dropped a size — stiffening the shoulder in all directions. Despite the name, it can come to anyone aged 40–60.

It usually goes through 3 stages. ① Freezing (painful, 2–9 months): it hurts even at rest, worse at night. ② Frozen (4–12 months): pain eases but the shoulder is badly stiff. ③ Thawing (6–24 months): motion slowly returns.

Most cases eventually improve, but left alone they take 1–3 years. The goal of treatment is to shorten that time and minimize the stiffness with stage-appropriate care.

Why does it happen?

  • Exact cause often unclear (idiopathic is most common)
  • Diabetes (several times higher risk, sometimes both shoulders)
  • Thyroid disease
  • Long immobilization after a shoulder injury or surgery
  • Long-neglected other shoulder pain (e.g. rotator-cuff problems)

✅ Do you have these symptoms?

  • The shoulder stiffens in 'every' direction (especially rotating outward)
  • The arm won't go all the way up even with help (you can't, and neither can someone else)
  • Severe night pain so you can't lie on that side and sleep poorly
  • Can't tie your hair, scratch your back, or pull a wallet from a back pocket
  • Pain that started gradually with no clear injury
  • Washing your back in the shower or buckling a seatbelt gets harder

🔬 Self-observations you can do at home

Go slowly and stay within a pain-free range. These responses are observations, not a diagnosis.

🔍 External-rotation comparison (the key sign of frozen shoulder)

Keep both elbows at your sides bent 90° and open both hands outward at the same time.

For reference: If the affected side opens less than half of the other, frozen shoulder is likely. Limited external rotation is the hallmark that distinguishes it from other shoulder problems.

🔍 Passive elevation test

Lying down, have someone slowly raise the affected arm overhead (you stay relaxed).

For reference: If it won't go up past a certain angle even with help — as if hitting a hard wall — the capsule is stiff. This distinguishes it from weakness (a cuff tear).

🔍 Daily-motion check

Reach a hand up your back and compare how far the thumb reaches on each side.

For reference: If the affected hand stops far lower (around the buttock) than the other, that's limited internal rotation — very common in frozen shoulder.

🤲 Hands-on care — provided by a professional

🤲 Manual therapy — capsular stretch & joint mobilization

The therapist gently stretches the stiff capsule in each direction and restores joint glide. A core treatment in the frozen/thawing stages, progressing gradually at a 'painful but good' intensity.

🤲 Injection — intra-articular steroid (by a doctor)

In the painful stage (1), an injection that directly calms inflammation in the capsule is proven to greatly shorten the painful period. The earlier in the painful stage, the better.

🤲 Hydrodilatation / manipulation under anesthesia (procedures)

For a badly stiff shoulder, the capsule can be stretched with injected saline (hydrodilatation) or released under anesthesia. Exercise therapy must follow.

🏃 Active care — exercises you do yourself

🏃 Pendulum exercise

Bend forward, let the arm hang relaxed, and draw small circles. The safest starter in the painful stage.

⏱ 1–2 minutes, 3–4 times a day

🏃 Stick-assisted exercise

Hold a long stick or umbrella with both hands and use the healthy arm to push the affected arm forward, out to the side, and into external rotation. Go to a 'stretch, pain 3' and hold 10 seconds.

⏱ 10 reps each direction, 2–3 times a day

🏃 Finger wall-walk

Face a wall and walk your fingers up it slowly like climbing. Mark the height you reach each day to see progress.

⏱ 10 reps, 2–3 times a day

🏃 Towel stretch (internal rotation behind the back)

Hold a towel behind your back (healthy hand on top) and pull it up with the healthy arm so the affected arm rides up your back.

⏱ 10-second holds × 10 reps, twice a day

🏃 Bedtime heat + stretch routine

Warm the capsule with 15 minutes of heat, then do the stretches above — it works better and reduces night pain.

⏱ 15–20 minutes before bed

🚨 Stop self-care and see a doctor if…

  • You have diabetes and the shoulder is starting to stiffen (it can progress fast and severely — treat early)
  • Sudden inability to raise the arm after trauma (rule out a tear/fracture)
  • Fever with a red, swollen shoulder (suspect septic arthritis — emergency)
  • Weight loss, night sweats, or other whole-body symptoms
  • Stiffness that keeps worsening despite 6+ months of active treatment
