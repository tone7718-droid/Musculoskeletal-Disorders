Carpal Tunnel Syndrome

The median nerve is squeezed in a narrow tunnel in the wrist, so the thumb to middle finger tingle and your hand wakes you at night.

✨ Open the interactive version (symptom check · compare)

📖 What is this condition?

Inside the wrist is a narrow tunnel (the carpal tunnel) bounded by wrist bones and a ligament, carrying 9 finger-flexing tendons and one nerve — the median nerve — together. Like a packed rush-hour train, if the tendons swell or the tunnel narrows, the weakest passenger, the nerve, gets squeezed.

The median nerve carries sensation for the thumb, index, middle, and half the ring finger, so when squeezed exactly those fingers tingle. Characteristically it's worse at night, because a bent wrist held during sleep raises tunnel pressure. Common in middle-aged women, pregnant women, and hand-intensive jobs — and the most common cause of hand tingling.

Why does it happen?

  • Repeated wrist use bent or extended (keyboard/mouse, dishwashing, hair work, assembly)
  • Swelling from pregnancy or menopausal hormone change
  • Diabetes, hypothyroidism, rheumatoid arthritis
  • Tunnel shape change after a wrist fracture or trauma
  • A genetically narrow tunnel

✅ Do you have these symptoms?

  • Tingling and burning in the thumb, index, and middle fingers (the little finger is spared — a key sign)
  • Hand tingling that wakes you at night/early morning, relieved by shaking the hand
  • Tingling after long gripping (steering wheel, phone)
  • Fine tasks getting clumsy (buttoning, picking up coins)
  • Frequently dropping things
  • (Advanced) the fleshy muscle at the thumb base looks flattened

🔬 Self-observations you can do at home

Go slowly and stay within a pain-free range. These responses are observations, not a diagnosis.

🔍 Phalen's test

Press the backs of your hands together in front of your chest with wrists bent 90° and hold for 60 seconds.

For reference: Reproduced tingling/numbness in the thumb–middle fingers within 60 seconds is positive.

🔍 Tinel's sign

Lightly tap the middle of the wrist crease on the palm side.

For reference: An electric feeling shooting into the thumb–middle fingers when tapped is positive.

🔍 Mapping the tingling

Mark exactly which fingers tingle.

For reference: Thumb–middle fingers points to the carpal tunnel; ring/little fingers to the ulnar nerve (elbow); the whole hand plus arm to a neck disc. The location is the diagnostic map.

🤲 Hands-on care — provided by a professional

🤲 Wrist splint (night brace)

A brace holding the wrist straight at night lowers tunnel pressure and reduces night tingling. A proven first-line treatment for early/mid carpal tunnel.

🤲 Manual therapy — nerve/tendon gliding, carpal-bone mobilization

The therapist glides the median nerve and tendons smoothly through the tunnel and mobilizes stiff wrist-bone joints to make tunnel space.

🤲 Injection (by a doctor)

A steroid injection into the tunnel calms tendon swelling for weeks to months. If symptoms are severe or muscle wasting begins, surgery (opening the tunnel ligament) may be needed, with generally good results.

🏃 Active care — exercises you do yourself

🏃 Tendon gliding exercise

Move the fingers slowly through ① straight → ② hook → ③ fist → ④ tabletop → ⑤ straight again. This glides the tunnel tendons evenly and prevents adhesions.

⏱ 5–10 cycles, 3 times a day

🏃 Median nerve gliding

Reach the arm to the side, palm up, and bend the wrist back while tilting the head away, then release. Only within a gentle range that doesn't worsen tingling.

⏱ 10 reps × 2 sets, twice a day

🏃 Building a neutral-wrist habit

Use a wrist rest at the keyboard, adjust mouse position, and avoid gripping the phone long — reduce time spent with the wrist bent at work. As important as the exercises.

⏱ Check your setup + relax the wrist every 30 minutes

🏃 Forearm stretch

Reach the arm out palm-up and bend the fingers down 30 sec, then palm-down and bend the wrist 30 sec — stretch the front and back of the forearm.

⏱ 30 sec each × 2 reps, 2–3 times a day

🏃 Grip recovery (after symptoms ease)

Squeeze and release a soft sponge ball gently and repeatedly, at an intensity that doesn't reproduce tingling.

⏱ 10 reps × 2 sets, once a day

🚨 Stop self-care and see a doctor if…

  • The bulge at the thumb base looks flatter than the other side (muscle wasting — see a doctor quickly)
  • Tingling that doesn't ease with shaking and lasts all day
  • Dulled sensation so you can't feel something hot well
  • No improvement after 3+ months of splinting/injection
  • Both hands tingle with neck pain (rule out a neck disc)
