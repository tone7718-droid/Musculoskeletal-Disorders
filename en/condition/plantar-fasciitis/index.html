Plantar Fasciitis (sole pain)

The bowstring-like fascia of the sole wears from overload, so the heel feels like it's tearing on the first morning step.

✨ Open the interactive version (symptom check · compare)

📖 What is this condition?

The plantar fascia is a tough sheet crossing the sole from heel to toes — it tensions the arch like a bowstring and acts as a spring carrying body weight with each step. When thousands of daily steps exceed its healing capacity, the fascia weakens where it attaches to the heel bone. Despite the '-itis' name, the essence is degeneration more than inflammation, so anti-inflammatories alone don't cure it.

The hallmark is 'first-step morning pain.' Overnight the foot points down and the fascia shortens; the first morning step suddenly loads and pulls it, so it feels like tearing. A few steps loosen it, but standing up after long sitting brings it back. Common in people who stand all day, runners, those who gained weight suddenly, or those with stiff calves.

Why does it happen?

  • Standing jobs, a sudden increase in walking/running
  • Short calf/Achilles (the most common companion problem)
  • Weight gain, pregnancy
  • Flat or high arches, unsupportive shoes, barefoot walking
  • Long activity on hard surfaces

✅ Do you have these symptoms?

  • Tearing pain in the inner heel on the first step in the morning
  • Pain eases after a few steps
  • Pain returns when standing up after long sitting
  • Aching at night after long standing or walking
  • A tender spot pressing the inner-bottom heel
  • Pulling sole pain when bending the toes up

🔬 Self-observations you can do at home

Go slowly and stay within a pain-free range. These responses are observations, not a diagnosis.

🔍 First-step morning pain check

Note the location and intensity of the first few steps' pain right after waking.

For reference: Inner-bottom heel pain worst on the first step and easing as you walk is classic plantar fasciitis.

🔍 Tender spot + toe-extension (windlass test)

Press the inner-bottom heel with your thumb, then extend the big toe up and press the same spot again.

For reference: If pain worsens with the toe extended (fascia tightened), it's likely.

🔍 Calf flexibility check

Do a wall calf-stretch with the knee straight and compare dorsiflexion both sides.

For reference: If the affected calf is stiffer, calf shortening is likely overloading the fascia — calf stretching becomes a core prescription.

🤲 Hands-on care — provided by a professional

🤲 Shockwave (ESWT)

The signature treatment for chronic plantar fasciitis — shockwaves to the worn attachment stimulate healing. Usually once a week for several sessions alongside exercise.

🤲 Manual therapy — calf/sole release, joint mobilization

The therapist releases the stiff calf and sole muscles and mobilizes stiff ankle/foot joints to reduce the pull on the fascia.

🤲 Night splint / taping / insoles

A night splint holding the ankle at 90° reduces first-step morning pain. Arch-supporting insoles and taping also help spread the load.

🤲 Steroid injection — cautious (ask a doctor)

Considered for severe pain, but repeated injections risk fascia rupture and heel fat-pad atrophy, so use is limited.

🏃 Active care — exercises you do yourself

🏃 In-bed stretch before getting up (preventing first-step pain)

Before standing, loop a towel around the sole and pull the toes toward you, holding 30 seconds, then take the first step.

⏱ 30 sec × 3 reps, every morning

🏃 Plantar-fascia self-stretch

Sitting, put the sore foot on the opposite knee, pull the toes up by hand to tense the sole, then massage the sole.

⏱ 30 sec × 3 reps, 3 times a day

🏃 Calf stretch (knee straight + bent)

Push a wall with the back knee straight 30 seconds (gastrocnemius) and slightly bent 30 seconds (soleus). One of the most proven exercises for plantar fasciitis.

⏱ 30 sec each × 3 reps, twice a day

🏃 Towel-under-toes calf raise (high-load strengthening)

Stand on a step's edge with a rolled towel under the toes to extend them, and do calf raises 3 seconds up, 3 seconds down. Rebuilds the fascia stronger.

⏱ 10 reps × 3 sets, every other day

🏃 Frozen-bottle roll + foot intrinsic exercise

On a bad-pain day, roll a frozen bottle under the sole 5 minutes to calm it, and scrunch a towel with the toes to wake the foot's small muscles.

⏱ Bottle 5 min + towel 2 min, 1–2 times a day

🚨 Stop self-care and see a doctor if…

  • Pain at rest and at night (rule out a stress fracture / other condition)
  • Pain at the back of the heel (not the bottom) (an Achilles problem — see that page)
  • Tingling or burning (rule out nerve entrapment)
  • Both feet hurt at once with other joints involved (rule out inflammatory disease)
  • A sudden 'pop' with bruising (fascia rupture)
