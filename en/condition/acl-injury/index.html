🦿 Knee

Anterior Cruciate Ligament (ACL) Injury

The key stabilizing ligament of the knee ruptures from a jump landing, sudden stop, or twist, leaving the knee unstable.

✨ Open the interactive version (symptom check · compare)

📖 What is this condition?

The ACL runs through the center of the knee, keeping the shin bone from sliding forward or twisting. It ruptures when the knee caves inward and twists — landing from a jump, stopping abruptly, cutting — often with a 'pop,' the knee shifting, and quick, large swelling.

Common in basketball, soccer, skiing, and handball, and notably more frequent in female athletes (linked to a tendency for the knee to cave inward when landing/jumping). A hallmark is that most happen in non-contact situations (no collision) — which is also the hope, since prevention training can lower the risk.

Treatment depends on activity level. Returning to jumping/cutting sport usually favors ligament reconstruction surgery; with low activity, non-surgical strengthening (especially hamstrings and glutes) to compensate is possible. Either way, rehab exercise decides the outcome.

Why does it happen?

  • The knee caving inward and twisting on landing, sudden stops, or cutting
  • Mostly non-contact (happens even without collision)
  • Poor landing/cutting technique, weak hips/hamstrings
  • Higher rate in women (anatomical, hormonal, movement-pattern factors)
  • High-risk sports — skiing, soccer, basketball

✅ Do you have these symptoms?

  • A 'pop' and the knee shifting at the moment of injury
  • Large swelling within hours (bleeding into the joint)
  • Unstable knee, fearful and buckling under weight
  • A feeling of the knee giving out when turning or going downhill
  • Difficulty fully straightening or bending the knee (swelling/other injury)
  • Afterward, fear of exercise because the knee feels 'untrustworthy'

🔬 Self-observations you can do at home

Go slowly and stay within a pain-free range. These responses are observations, not a diagnosis.

🔍 Mechanism & sound check

Recall the situation (jump landing, sudden stop, twist), a 'pop,' and how fast it swelled.

For reference: Non-contact twist + 'pop' + rapid swelling within hours makes an ACL injury likely. Here, a prompt orthopedic visit matters more than self-tests.

🔍 Instability check

(After swelling/pain settle) walk slowly on flat ground and change direction slightly. Don't force it.

For reference: A giving-way feeling suggests instability. The actual test (Lachman, etc.) is done by a specialist.

🔍 Companion-injury possibility

Check for joint-line pain (meniscus) or inner-ligament-area pain.

For reference: ACL injuries often come with meniscus/MCL injuries, so a comprehensive evaluation is needed.

🤲 Hands-on care — provided by a professional

🤲 Acute care & accurate diagnosis

Right after injury, protect, ice, compress, and elevate to manage swelling, and see an orthopedist with an MRI as soon as possible to confirm the ACL and any companion injuries.

🤲 Pre-surgery rehab (prehab) — manual/physical therapy

Even with planned surgery, first reducing swelling, fully straightening the knee, and restoring thigh strength greatly improves the surgical outcome. The therapist aids range and strength recovery.

🤲 Reconstruction & staged rehab (medical team)

For sport return, reconstruction surgery is followed by months of staged rehab. Return is decided by strength/function tests passing — not by time.

🏃 Active care — exercises you do yourself

🏃 Early — range of motion & waking the thigh

Fully straighten the knee, heel-slide to bend, and lie down for straight-leg raises to wake the thigh muscles asleep under the swelling.

⏱ Daily under a physical therapist's guidance

🏃 Mid — hamstring & glute strengthening

Glute bridges, hamstring curls, step-ups to focus on the hamstrings and glutes that back up the ACL. Keep the knee from caving inward.

⏱ 3 times a week per the rehab stage

🏃 Late — balance, jump & landing re-education

Single-leg balance, two-foot → single-foot jumps, soft landings (knee over the toes, not caving in) to restore neuromuscular control.

⏱ Staged, under expert guidance

🏃 Prevention — jump/cutting program

(Prevention/recurrence) A neuromuscular program of proper landing/cutting technique plus hip strengthening genuinely lowers ACL injury risk.

⏱ 2–3 times a week in season

🚨 Stop self-care and see a doctor if…

  • Large swelling and inability to bear weight right after injury (needs prompt care)
  • The knee locks and won't straighten (rule out a meniscus injury)
  • Repeated dislocation/buckling making daily life unstable
  • Tingling or pale, cold foot/calf (nerve/vessel injury — emergency)
  • Another 'pop' and instability during rehab (rule out a re-tear)

⚠️ This material is for health information only and does not replace a doctor's diagnosis or treatment. If symptoms last more than 2 weeks or any red flag applies, see a medical professional.
