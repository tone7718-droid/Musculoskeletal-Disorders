Shoulder Impingement Syndrome

When you raise the arm, the space between the roof bone and the arm bone narrows and a tendon gets pinched — the most common shoulder pain.

✨ Open the interactive version (symptom check · compare)

📖 What is this condition?

In the shoulder there's a narrow tunnel between a roof-like bone (acromion) and the arm bone, and the rotator-cuff tendon and a fluid sac (bursa) pass through it. Each time you raise the arm this tunnel narrows. With poor posture or disordered blade movement, the tendon inside gets pinched repeatedly — like a sleeve caught in a door.

At first it only twinges at a certain angle, but repeated pinching inflames the tendon and bursa until it aches even at rest. Left long enough, the tendon can wear down into a rotator-cuff tear, so catching it early matters.

Why does it happen?

  • A hunched back and rounded shoulders (the tunnel space itself narrows)
  • Scapular dyskinesis (the roof fails to move out of the way in time)
  • Rotator-cuff weakness letting the arm bone ride up and press the tunnel
  • Repeated overhead arm activity (swimming, badminton, painting, reaching shelves)
  • Age-related changes in acromion shape, bone spurs

✅ Do you have these symptoms?

  • Pain especially in the 60–120° arc when raising the arm out to the side (less above that)
  • Pain dressing, washing your hair, or reaching for a back pocket
  • Worse pain when lying on the affected side
  • Aching in the front/outer shoulder, spreading down the outer arm
  • A 'catching' feeling or sound when raising the arm
  • At first only with activity, then gradually even at rest

🔬 Self-observations you can do at home

Go slowly and stay within a pain-free range. These responses are observations, not a diagnosis.

🔍 Painful arc test

Slowly raise the affected arm out to the side all the way up, then lower it.

For reference: Pain only in the 60–120° band (around shoulder height) and less above or below is the classic sign of impingement.

🔍 Hawkins test (safe version)

Raise the arm 90° forward with the elbow bent 90°, then slowly rotate the arm inward so the back of the hand faces the floor.

For reference: If your familiar pain appears in the front/outer shoulder, impingement is likely.

🔍 Scapular assistance test

Have someone support the lower blade and help it rotate upward while you raise the arm.

For reference: If pain eases with the help, correcting blade movement becomes the core treatment (see the scapular dyskinesis page).

🤲 Hands-on care — provided by a professional

🤲 Manual therapy — joint mobilization & soft-tissue release

The therapist glides the arm-bone head slightly downward to make tunnel space, and releases the stiff posterior capsule and chest muscles to create alignment with less impingement.

🤲 Shockwave / ultrasound therapy

Boosts blood flow to the inflamed tendon and stimulates healing. Exercise must go alongside while pain eases, or it will come back.

🤲 Injection (by a doctor)

If pain is bad enough to disturb sleep, a bursa anti-inflammatory injection can put out the fire first. The injection 'creates a chance to exercise' — it isn't the end of treatment.

🏃 Active care — exercises you do yourself

🏃 Band external rotation (rotator-cuff strengthening)

Keep the elbow at your side bent 90°, hold a band, and slowly rotate the hand outward. Tucking a towel between elbow and side improves form.

⏱ 15 reps × 3 sets, once a day

🏃 Posterior capsule stretch (sleeper stretch)

Lie on the affected side, reach that arm forward 90° with elbow bent 90°. With the other hand, gently press the wrist toward the floor to stretch the back of the shoulder. Only to a 'stretch,' not pain.

⏱ 30 sec × 3 reps, 1–2 times a day

🏃 Wall slides

Face a wall with both elbows and the pinky-sides of the hands on it, and slide the arms up along the wall. Raise and lower only within a pain-free range.

⏱ 10 reps × 3 sets, once a day

🏃 Serratus / lower-trap strengthening (wall push-up plus, Y-raise)

Same as the scapular dyskinesis page. Half of impingement is a blade problem, so always do these together.

⏱ 10 reps each × 2–3 sets, once a day

🏃 Posture reset (chest opener + chin tuck)

Every hour, stand, open the chest, tuck the chin, and rotate both arms outward, holding 10 seconds. The simplest way to widen the tunnel space.

⏱ 10 sec × 3 reps, every hour

🚨 Stop self-care and see a doctor if…

  • A sudden, severe loss of strength so you can't raise the arm (suspect a tear)
  • Night pain waking you for more than 2 weeks
  • The shoulder stiffening in all directions (suspect frozen shoulder)
  • Shape change or severe swelling after trauma
  • No improvement at all after 6–8 weeks of exercise therapy

⚠️ This material is for health information only and does not replace a doctor's diagnosis or treatment. If symptoms last more than 2 weeks or any red flag applies, see a medical professional.
