🦵 Hip · Thigh

Greater Trochanteric Pain Syndrome (outer-hip pain)

The gluteus tendons attaching to the bony point on the outer hip weaken and get irritated, so lying on that side hurts.

✨ Open the interactive version (symptom check · compare)

📖 What is this condition?

Feel the upper-outer thigh and you'll find a bony point (the greater trochanter). The tendons of important muscles (gluteus medius/minimus) that hold the pelvis level attach here. When these tendons get worn and irritated by repeated compression and overload, it's greater trochanteric pain syndrome. It used to be called 'bursitis,' but the real lead is the tendon (gluteal tendinopathy), not the bursa.

The hallmark is 'pain lying down.' Lying on the affected side presses the bone directly; lying on the other side drops the upper leg and pulls the tendon — both hurt. So it's a condition that disturbs sleep. Especially common in middle-aged women, because a wider pelvis makes the tendon bend and compress more over the bone.

Why does it happen?

  • Weak glutes plus a sudden activity increase (hiking, stairs, starting to run)
  • Crossing the legs or standing on one leg — postures that compress the tendon over the bone
  • Middle-aged women (hormonal change and pelvic structure)
  • A habit of sleeping only on one side
  • Back or knee pain changing the gait so one side overworks

✅ Do you have these symptoms?

  • Exact tenderness pressing the bony point on the outer hip
  • Lying on the affected side hurts so much sleep is disturbed
  • Pain on the outer hip climbing stairs, going uphill, standing on one leg
  • Worse after long standing or walking
  • Pain sometimes spreading down the outer thigh toward the knee
  • Discomfort sitting with the legs crossed

🔬 Self-observations you can do at home

Go slowly and stay within a pain-free range. These responses are observations, not a diagnosis.

🔍 Greater trochanter tenderness test

Lie on your side and press the bony point on the upper-outer thigh with a finger.

For reference: If pressing reproduces your usual pain exactly, it's likely. (Deep-middle buttock pain points to the piriformis; groin pain to an intra-joint hip problem.)

🔍 30-second single-leg stand test

Stand only on the affected leg for 30 seconds.

For reference: Outer-hip pain appearing within 30 seconds strongly suggests a gluteal tendon problem.

🔍 Sleep-position check

Note which sleeping position hurts.

For reference: If lying on the affected side hurts (compression) and lying on the other side also hurts (stretch), that 'both sides uncomfortable' pattern is classic.

🤲 Hands-on care — provided by a professional

🤲 Shockwave (ESWT)

Shockwaves to the worn gluteal tendon stimulate healing. A signature treatment with reported benefit for chronic gluteal tendinopathy.

🤲 Manual therapy — surrounding release & gait correction

The therapist releases the stiff outer-thigh fascia and the back/pelvis area, and corrects a gait where the pelvis drops to the side, reducing the tendon's load.

🤲 Injection (by a doctor)

An injection around the tendon can calm severe pain. Short-term relief is good, but without exercise it commonly recurs.

🤲 Sleep-setup adjustment

A pillow between the knees when lying on the other side keeps the upper leg from dropping, reducing tendon pull and night pain.

🏃 Active care — exercises you do yourself

🏃 Isometric hip abduction (pain-calming stage)

Lie with knees bent and a band (or your hands) outside the knees; push outward 5–10 seconds without moving the legs.

⏱ 10 sec × 10 reps, twice a day

🏃 Clamshell

Lie on your side (affected side up) with knees bent, and slowly open and close the top knee. Keep the pelvis from rolling back.

⏱ 15 reps × 3 sets, once a day

🏃 Side-lying leg raise → progress to wall slides

Lie on your side and slowly raise the straight top leg slightly behind the diagonal, then lower. As it gets easier, progress to standing wall-push exercises.

⏱ 12 reps × 3 sets, once a day

🏃 Step-up (stair-climb exercise)

Put the affected foot on a low step and slowly step up and down without letting the pelvis drop to the side. A mirror to check level hips helps.

⏱ 10 reps × 3 sets, once a day

🏃 Cutting compressive postures

Avoid crossing the legs, standing on one leg, and lying on the affected side. For this tendon, 'stretching' is often harmful, so skip stretches that pull the leg across your body for now.

⏱ Lifestyle — always

🚨 Stop self-care and see a doctor if…

  • Can't bear weight after a fall (rule out a hip fracture — especially the elderly)
  • Groin pain is the main symptom (rule out an intra-joint hip problem)
  • Fever or pain persisting day and night
  • No improvement after 3+ months of diligent exercise

⚠️ This material is for health information only and does not replace a doctor's diagnosis or treatment. If symptoms last more than 2 weeks or any red flag applies, see a medical professional.
