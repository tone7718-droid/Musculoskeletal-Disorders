🦶 Ankle · Foot

Hallux Valgus (Bunion)

The big toe drifts toward the second toe and its base joint juts inward, so shoes become painful — a deformity condition.

✨ Open the interactive version (symptom check · compare)

📖 What is this condition?

Hallux valgus is a deformity where the big toe bends toward the second toe and its base joint juts the other way, inward. The protruding part rubs in shoes and gets red, swollen, and painful — but that's not all. The big toe is the 'engine' that pushes off the ground last when walking; a bent big toe can't do this job well, so calluses and pain form under the second and third toes and the whole foot's balance breaks down.

It's built on a genetic base (an inherited foot shape) with shoes — narrow, high-heeled — as the accelerant. It's overwhelmingly more common in women. A bent bone won't straighten back with exercise, but reducing pain and slowing progression with exercise and shoe care is quite possible.

Why does it happen?

  • Genetics (a family history is very common)
  • Long-term wear of narrow shoes and high heels
  • Flat or overly flexible feet
  • Joint diseases such as rheumatoid arthritis
  • Weak foot intrinsic muscles

✅ Do you have these symptoms?

  • The big-toe base joint juts inward
  • The protrusion rubs in shoes and is red and painful (bursitis)
  • The big toe bends toward the second toe and seems to be worsening
  • Calluses and pain forming under the second/third toes
  • Pain across the front of the foot after long walking
  • Increasing difficulty finding shoes that fit

🔬 Self-observations you can do at home

Go slowly and stay within a pain-free range. These responses are observations, not a diagnosis.

🔍 Watch the angle

Stand barefoot, look down at your foot, and take a photo. Note the angle between the big toe and the inner foot line.

For reference: If the big toe is clearly bent toward the second toe and has progressed compared to a photo 6–12 months ago, it needs managing. (The exact angle is measured by X-ray.)

🔍 Shoe-outline test

Place your bare foot on top of your usual shoe.

For reference: If the foot outline spills past the shoe width, that shoe is pushing the deformity every day.

🔍 Big-toe function test

Stand barefoot and try to lift only the big toe off the floor, then press only the big toe down while lifting the others.

For reference: If you can't control the big toe separately, the foot intrinsics are 'asleep' — meaning there's room to wake them with exercise.

🤲 Hands-on care — provided by a professional

🤲 Shoe correction (the most effective conservative care)

Switching to wide-toe, low-heel (under 3 cm) shoes is more effective than any treatment. Shop in the afternoon (when feet swell) and choose by toe width.

🤲 Aids — toe spacers, night splint, insoles

A silicone spacer between the big and second toes, a night correction splint, and arch-supporting insoles help reduce pain and slow progression. (They won't straighten an already-bent bone.)

🤲 Manual / physical therapy

The therapist gently mobilizes the stiff big-toe joint and releases the sole fascia to reduce pain. For inflammation of the protrusion (bursitis), ice and padding reduce friction.

🤲 Surgery (for severe deformity)

If pain disrupts daily life despite conservative care, surgery to cut and realign the bone is considered. The criterion is 'pain and function,' not 'looks.'

🏃 Active care — exercises you do yourself

🏃 Short-foot exercise (building the arch)

Sitting or standing, without curling the toes, draw the sole's arch up short while keeping the big-toe base and heel on the floor, holding 5 seconds. A foot 'core' exercise.

⏱ 5 sec × 10 reps × 2 sets, twice a day

🏃 Toe-spreading exercise

Barefoot, fan the five toes wide apart and bring them back. If it's hard, slide fingers between the toes to wake the sensation, then try again.

⏱ 10 reps × 3 sets, twice a day

🏃 Big-toe outward-pull stretch

Hold the big toe and gently pull it the opposite (inward) way, holding 30 seconds. A thick rubber band looped around both big toes to pull them apart also works.

⏱ 30 sec × 3 reps, twice a day

🏃 Towel scrunch + marble pickup

Scrunch a towel with the toes and pick up small marbles or a sock with the toes. Wakes all the foot intrinsics.

⏱ 2 min each, once a day

🏃 Calf raise — push toward the big toe

When doing calf raises, practice driving weight toward the big and second toes rather than letting it leak to the little-toe side. Re-educates the final push-off of walking.

⏱ 15 reps × 3 sets, every other day

🚨 Stop self-care and see a doctor if…

  • The protrusion is severely swollen, red, and hot (rule out infection/gout)
  • The big toe bends fast and starts to overlap the second toe (above or below)
  • Front-foot pain so severe walking itself is hard
  • You have diabetes and a wound or blister forms on the foot (diabetic foot care — see a doctor immediately)
  • Pain disrupting daily life despite 6 months of conservative care (surgery consult)
