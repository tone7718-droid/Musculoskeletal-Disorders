Tennis Elbow (Lateral Epicondylitis)

The tendon of the wrist-extending muscles wears down where it attaches to the outer elbow bone, so gripping and lifting hurt.

✨ Open the interactive version (symptom check · compare)

📖 What is this condition?

The bony bump on the outside of the elbow is where the tendons of the muscles that extend and lift the wrist attach. When small injuries pile up repeatedly, healing can't keep up and the tendon weakens like an old rubber band. Despite the '-itis' name, the essence is tendon degeneration (wear) more than inflammation — so rest alone often doesn't heal it.

Despite the name, most patients don't play tennis. Any repetitive wrist task — mouse and keyboard work, lifting a frying pan, wringing a cloth, turning a screwdriver — can cause it. It's the most common elbow condition in ages 30–50.

Why does it happen?

  • Repeated wrist extension and gripping (mouse, cooking, cleaning, tools)
  • A sudden jump in hand use (moving house, big-batch cooking, a new sport)
  • A habit of lifting heavy things with a straight elbow
  • Faulty form in racquet sports like a tennis backhand
  • Age-related drop in tendon healing (peaks around the 40s)

✅ Do you have these symptoms?

  • Pain when you press the bony bump on the outer elbow
  • A twinge in the outer elbow when gripping and lifting (kettle, frying pan, coffee pot)
  • Pain wringing a cloth or towel
  • Pain shaking hands or turning a doorknob
  • Pain radiating from the elbow down the outer forearm
  • If severe, aching even at rest and a weak grip

🔬 Self-observations you can do at home

Go slowly and stay within a pain-free range. These responses are observations, not a diagnosis.

🔍 Chair-lift test

With the elbow straight and the back of the hand up, lift a chair slightly by the backrest with one hand.

For reference: If your familiar pain appears on the outer elbow, tennis elbow is likely.

🔍 Cozen's test (resisted wrist extension)

With the elbow straight and a fist, extend the wrist up. Resist as someone presses the back of your hand down.

For reference: Pain at the outer elbow bone while resisting is positive.

🔍 Middle-finger resistance test

With the elbow and fingers straight, resist as someone presses your middle finger down.

For reference: Reproduced pain on the outer elbow is positive. If tingling is present, the nerve must be ruled out.

🤲 Hands-on care — provided by a professional

🤲 Shockwave (ESWT)

Shockwaves to the worn tendon area stimulate blood flow and healing. The standard non-surgical treatment for tennis elbow that's chronic for 6+ months.

🤲 Manual therapy — friction massage & joint mobilization

The therapist applies cross-friction massage at the tendon attachment to stimulate healing, and releases the stiff elbow/wrist joints and forearm muscles to reduce the tendon's load.

🤲 Brace (elbow strap)

A strap worn about 5 cm below the elbow spreads the pull on the tendon and eases daily pain. Wear it during work and remove it for sleep.

🤲 Injection (by a doctor)

Steroid injections help short-term pain but have more recurrences long-term, so they're used cautiously. Newer injections (prolotherapy, PRP) aimed at tendon healing are sometimes used.

🏃 Active care — exercises you do yourself

🏃 Isometric wrist extension (pain-calming stage)

Support the elbow at 90°, hold the wrist slightly extended, and resist with the other hand pressing the back of the hand so it doesn't move. Keep intensity at pain 3 or under.

⏱ 30–45 sec × 5 reps, twice a day

🏃 Eccentric wrist drop (the core tennis-elbow exercise)

Rest the forearm on a table with the wrist over the edge, holding a light dumbbell (0.5–1 kg) or water bottle. Help lift it with the other hand, then lower it slowly over 3–4 seconds with the sore hand alone. Slow lowering rebuilds the tendon.

⏱ 15 reps × 3 sets, once a day (reduce weight if next-day pain worsens)

🏃 Tyler twist (rubber-bar twist)

If you have a flex-bar: hold it upright with the sore hand, twist the top with the other hand, then slowly untwist with the sore wrist. A research-proven exercise.

⏱ 15 reps × 3 sets, once a day

🏃 Forearm extensor stretch

With the elbow straight and the back of the hand down, gently pull the back of the hand toward you with the other hand to stretch the outer forearm.

⏱ 30 sec × 3 reps, 2–3 times a day

🏃 Grip & shoulder strengthening (final stage)

As pain eases, expand to soft-ball squeezes, light dumbbells, and band shoulder work. A weak shoulder and blade overload the wrist tendon, so strengthen them too.

⏱ 3 times a week

🚨 Stop self-care and see a doctor if…

  • A swollen, warm, red elbow
  • Tingling or altered sensation in the fingers (rule out nerve compression)
  • Inability to bend or straighten the elbow itself (rule out a joint problem)
  • No improvement after 6+ months of diligent exercise therapy
  • Severe night pain and a steadily weakening grip
