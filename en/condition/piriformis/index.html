Piriformis Syndrome (deep buttock pain)

Piriformis Syndrome / Deep Gluteal Syndrome

The deep piriformis muscle irritates the sciatic nerve, so the buttock and leg tingle even though it isn't a disc.

✨ Open the interactive version (symptom check · compare)

📖 What is this condition?

The piriformis is a small muscle deep in the buttock connecting the pelvis to the thigh bone. The problem is its location — the thick sciatic nerve heading to the leg runs right beneath it (between it, in some people). When the piriformis stiffens or swells from overwork, it squeezes the sciatic nerve and the buttock and back of the leg tingle just like a disc.

The difference from a lumbar disc: the back itself doesn't hurt much, and the pain centers 'deep in the middle of the buttock.' Sitting long (especially hard chairs, the driver's seat) worsens it, and sitting is more distressing than bending the back. Recently it's also called 'deep gluteal syndrome' to include nearby structures.

Why does it happen?

  • Long sitting (driving, desk work) — the piriformis works while compressed
  • Sitting on a wallet in a back pocket
  • Weak glutes overworking the small piriformis
  • A sudden increase in running or hiking
  • Direct bruise from falling on the buttocks
  • An anatomical variant where the sciatic nerve pierces the piriformis

✅ Do you have these symptoms?

  • Deep pain in the middle of the buttock, achy when pressed
  • Tingling down the buttock and back of the thigh after long sitting
  • Driving or hard chairs especially distressing
  • The back doesn't hurt much
  • Tightness in the buttock climbing stairs or squatting
  • Little or no pain shooting down the leg with cough/sneeze

🔬 Self-observations you can do at home

Go slowly and stay within a pain-free range. These responses are observations, not a diagnosis.

🔍 FAIR test (piriformis stretch test)

Lie on the unaffected side and bring the affected bent knee toward the chest and across toward the opposite floor to stretch the piriformis.

For reference: Reproduced deep buttock pain or leg tingling suggests piriformis irritation.

🔍 Seated tenderness check

Place a tennis ball on a chair and sit so it presses the middle of the buttock.

For reference: If your familiar pain and leg tingling appear when the ball makes contact, it suggests a piriformis-area problem.

🔍 Telling it apart from a disc

Bend the back forward while standing (a disc-provoking move), then sit with weight on the painful buttock (a piriformis-provoking move).

For reference: If bending is fine but sitting/buttock pressure hurts more, it leans toward the piriformis. If bending strongly tingles the leg, check the lumbar-disc page.

🤲 Hands-on care — provided by a professional

🤲 Manual therapy — deep piriformis release & neural mobilization

The therapist releases the deep buttock muscle directly and glides the sciatic nerve so it slides smoothly between tissues. Surface massage can't reach this depth, so professional care is effective.

🤲 Shockwave / dry needling

Shockwave or needling to the stiff piriformis trigger point releases tension and improves blood flow.

🤲 Injection (by a doctor)

Under ultrasound, an injection around the piriformis calms muscle tension and nerve-area inflammation. It can also help confirm the diagnosis.

🏃 Active care — exercises you do yourself

🏃 Piriformis stretch (figure-4 stretch)

Lie down, place the affected ankle on the opposite knee in a figure-4, and hug the lower thigh toward the chest. Stretch to a comfortable pull deep in the buttock.

⏱ 30 sec × 3 reps, 2–3 times a day

🏃 Tennis-ball self-release

Lie with a tennis ball under the buttock and rest on a 'good-hurt' spot for 30–60 seconds. Move it slightly or stop if tingling worsens.

⏱ 30–60 sec × 2–3 spots, 1–2 times a day

🏃 Glute strengthening (bridge, clamshell)

Wake the large glutes with bridges and clamshells. When the big muscles do the work, the small piriformis is spared overwork — the key to preventing recurrence.

⏱ 15 reps × 3 sets each, once a day

🏃 Sciatic nerve glide

Sitting, extend the affected leg forward as you lift the head, then bend it as you drop the head, gently and repeatedly.

⏱ 10 reps × 2 sets, twice a day

🏃 Changing how you sit

Stand every 30 minutes, use a soft cushion, remove the back-pocket wallet, and stretch at rest stops every hour when driving. Reducing compression time is half the treatment.

⏱ Lifestyle — always

🚨 Stop self-care and see a doctor if…

  • Leg tingling worse when bending the back, with cough sending pain down the leg (rule out a lumbar disc)
  • Weak or numb foot
  • Buttock pain with fever or severe night pain
  • No improvement after 8+ weeks of self-care

⚠️ This material is for health information only and does not replace a doctor's diagnosis or treatment. If symptoms last more than 2 weeks or any red flag applies, see a medical professional.
