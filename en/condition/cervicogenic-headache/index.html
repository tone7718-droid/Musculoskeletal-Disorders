🦒 Neck

Cervicogenic Headache (headache from the neck)

A problem in the upper neck joints and muscles travels along nerves to the head, causing a one-sided headache that starts at the back of the head.

✨ Open the interactive version (symptom check · compare)

📖 What is this condition?

Not every headache is a head problem. A cervicogenic headache starts in the joints and muscles of the top three neck bones (C1–C3); that pain links to the nerves going to the back and side of the head and is felt as a headache. It's 'pain the neck sends' echoing in the head, not the brain itself.

The features are clear. It's usually one-sided, starts at the back of the head/nape, and spreads to the crown or behind the eyes. Holding the neck a certain way for long or pressing the neck reproduces the headache. It's closely tied to forward head posture and long screen work, so it's common in office workers and students.

It's easily confused with migraine or tension headache, but the key clue is that it 'clearly links to neck movement and posture.' Treating the neck and the headache easing is both diagnosis and treatment.

Why does it happen?

  • Overload of the upper neck joints from forward head / straight neck
  • Long periods in one position (monitor, phone, reading)
  • Tension and trigger points in the suboccipital muscles (where head meets neck)
  • Sleep posture — high pillow, sleeping face-down
  • A history of whiplash (car accident)

✅ Do you have these symptoms?

  • A one-sided headache starting at the back of the head/nape and spreading to the front/eyes
  • Turning or tilting the neck triggers or worsens the headache
  • Pressing the junction of the nape and skull reproduces your familiar headache
  • Worse later in the day and the longer you work
  • Shoulder/neck stiffness comes with the headache
  • Painkillers help only briefly and changing posture changes it

🔬 Self-observations you can do at home

Go slowly and stay within a pain-free range. These responses are observations, not a diagnosis.

🔍 Suboccipital tender-point check

Press firmly with both thumbs at the junction of the back of the head and the neck (the hollow just under the skull).

For reference: If pressing reproduces your usual headache spreading up into the head, cervicogenic headache is likely.

🔍 Neck-posture link test

Slowly turn the head to one side or extend it back and hold 30 seconds, then hold a tucked-chin upright posture 30 seconds.

For reference: If a certain neck posture triggers the headache or upright posture reduces it, the neck is likely the cause.

🔍 Migraine rule-out check

Note any light/sound sensitivity, nausea, throbbing pain, or visual flashes.

For reference: If these are prominent, it may be a migraine — better to distinguish with a doctor than to self-manage.

🤲 Hands-on care — provided by a professional

🤲 Manual therapy — upper cervical mobilization

The therapist gently mobilizes the C1–C3 joints — the source of the headache — and releases the suboccipital muscles. The best-proven treatment for cervicogenic headache.

🤲 Myofascial / dry-needling (trigger point) treatment

Releasing trigger points in the nape, top of the shoulder, and suboccipital muscles reduces the referred pain spreading to the head.

🤲 Medication / nerve treatment (by a doctor)

For severe pain, short-term medication or an occipital nerve block may be considered — an aid to lower pain so exercise and posture work can start.

🏃 Active care — exercises you do yourself

🏃 Chin tuck + nodding exercise

Tuck the chin straight back, then make a small nod (gently drop and lift the head) using just the upper neck. The core exercise to wake the deep suboccipital muscles.

⏱ 10 reps × 3 sets, 2–3 times a day

🏃 Suboccipital self-release

Lie down with two massage balls (or balls wrapped in a towel) under the junction below the back of the head, and gently nod, releasing for 30–60 seconds.

⏱ 60 sec × 2 reps, once a day

🏃 Upper trapezius / levator scapulae stretch

Anchor the shoulder by holding a chair, and pull the head diagonally with the other hand to stretch the side of the neck.

⏱ 30 sec × 3 reps (each side), twice a day

🏃 Thoracic extension + posture reset

Every hour, stand, open the chest, and hold a tucked-chin posture 10 seconds. Raising the monitor to eye level matters as much as medication.

⏱ 10 sec × 3 reps, every hour

🚨 Stop self-care and see a doctor if…

  • A sudden 'worst headache of your life' (emergency)
  • Headache with slurred speech, one-sided weakness, severe dizziness, or vision loss (emergency)
  • Fever and neck stiffness so you can't bend your head forward (suspect infection)
  • A headache that keeps worsening, wakes you at dawn, or comes with vomiting
  • A new headache after age 50 or a pattern that suddenly changed
