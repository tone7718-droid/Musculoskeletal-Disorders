🦿 Knee

Knee Osteoarthritis (degenerative arthritis)

The knee cartilage wears and the whole joint changes, becoming painful and stiff — the most common knee condition after middle age.

✨ Open the interactive version (symptom check · compare)

📖 What is this condition?

Osteoarthritis isn't simply 'worn cartilage' but a change in the whole joint — cartilage, bone, lining, and muscle. Cartilage is the smooth cushion covering the bone ends; when years and load roughen and thin it, the joint stiffens and hurts to move. It's very common after middle age, especially in women and people with a lot of floor-sitting (squatting, sitting cross-legged).

Two must-know facts. First, X-ray severity doesn't match pain — many with bad images walk fine, and vice versa. Second, the idea that 'sparing it helps' is wrong. Cartilage gets its nutrition by being squeezed and released like a sponge, so appropriate exercise is cartilage's food. Every major guideline names 'exercise' — not drugs or injections — as the first-line treatment for osteoarthritis.

Why does it happen?

  • Age (cartilage losing water and elasticity)
  • Obesity — the knee takes 3–5× body weight (losing 5 kg cuts knee load 15–25 kg)
  • Floor-sitting habits — squatting, cross-legged, kneeling
  • Past knee injury (history of meniscus/ligament injury)
  • Weak thighs, bow-legs and other alignment issues, genetics

✅ Do you have these symptoms?

  • Pain going up and down stairs and squatting (worse going down)
  • Stiffness so you limp a few steps after long sitting (eases within 30 minutes)
  • Swelling and aching at night after a day of much walking
  • Creaking and grinding in the knee
  • Difficulty fully bending or straightening the knee
  • More aching in cloudy weather
  • If severe, the leg looks bowed

🔬 Self-observations you can do at home

Go slowly and stay within a pain-free range. These responses are observations, not a diagnosis.

🔍 Morning-stiffness duration

Note how many minutes the knee is stiff in the morning or after long sitting.

For reference: Easing within 30 minutes fits the osteoarthritis pattern. Lasting over an hour with several swollen joints needs a rheumatoid check.

🔍 30-second sit-to-stand test

Arms crossed, count how many times you can stand and sit from a chair in 30 seconds.

For reference: Under 12 for someone in their 60s suggests weak legs and urgent need for strengthening. Also a great recovery-tracking tool.

🔍 Pain diary

For 1–2 weeks, record which activities (stairs, shopping, hiking) bring on worse pain/swelling.

For reference: Repeated next-day swelling after a certain activity means that volume exceeds the knee's current limit — use it as a guide to 'manage the amount,' not to quit.

🤲 Hands-on care — provided by a professional

🤲 Manual/physical therapy — joint mobilization and heat

The therapist restores knee glide and releases surrounding muscles to reduce stiffness. Heat is a good warm-up step before exercise.

🤲 Medication / injection (by a doctor)

During painful flares, anti-inflammatories and intra-articular injections (hyaluronic acid, sometimes steroid) control pain. All are aids to create a 'body that can exercise.'

🤲 Assistive devices

A cane (in the hand opposite the painful knee), a knee sleeve, and cushioned shoes reduce pain and protect activity. Using aids isn't shameful — it's a smart strategy.

🤲 Surgery is the last card

When pain, weight, and exercise management still fail and daily life collapses, consider joint replacement. Even with surgery, stronger pre-op muscles mean better outcomes.

🏃 Active care — exercises you do yourself

🏃 Front-thigh strengthening (seated leg extension)

Sitting, slowly straighten one knee, hold 5 seconds, lower slowly. Thigh muscle is the knee's 'natural brace.' Add an ankle weight as it gets easier.

⏱ 10 reps × 3 sets (each side), 1–2 times a day

🏃 Sit-to-stand (everyday squat)

Stand and sit from a chair slowly without using armrests. Use a lower chair as it gets easier, a higher cushion if it's hard.

⏱ 10 reps × 3 sets, twice a day

🏃 Flat walking, or pool walking/cycling

Walk on flat ground at an intensity under pain 3. Pool walking and stationary cycling are excellent low-load alternatives on bad days.

⏱ 30 min (split into 10s is fine), 5 times a week

🏃 Hip & calf strengthening

Glute bridges and calf raises strengthen the joints above and below to share the knee's load.

⏱ 10–15 reps × 3 sets each, once a day

🏃 Weight management

If overweight, aim to lose 5–10% of body weight with diet and exercise. The most powerful 'medicine' for knee pain.

⏱ Long-term goal — 0.25–0.5 kg a week

🚨 Stop self-care and see a doctor if…

  • A suddenly very swollen, hot, red knee (infection/gout — see a doctor quickly)
  • The knee locking so it won't straighten, or buckling weakly
  • Sudden severe pain and swelling after trauma (rule out fracture/ligament)
  • Pain waking you at night that painkillers don't touch
  • Walking distance dropping sharply so daily life is hard (consider surgery consult)

⚠️ This material is for health information only and does not replace a doctor's diagnosis or treatment. If symptoms last more than 2 weeks or any red flag applies, see a medical professional.
