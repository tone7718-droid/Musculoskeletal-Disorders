🧍 Lower Back · Pelvis

Non-specific Low Back Pain (simple back pain / lumbar strain)

Pain from overload of muscles, ligaments, and small joints — without a serious structural problem like a disc or stenosis. About 90% of back pain.

✨ Open the interactive version (symptom check · compare)

📖 What is this condition?

Whenever the back hurts, everyone worries 'is it a disc?' But about 90% of back pain is 'non-specific' — from overload and strain of muscles, ligaments, and small joints, with no serious damage to a specific structure. Just as you can twist an ankle, you can tweak a back, or accumulated bad posture can make a muscle cry out.

The good news: most cases recover on their own within 2–6 weeks. The bad news: the old idea of 'absolute rest because it hurts' actually slows recovery. Modern medicine is clear — the sooner you move as normally as you can within tolerance, the faster you heal.

Even if an MRI shows disc degeneration, no need to panic. Many pain-free adults also show disc changes on MRI. Images and pain are more separate than you'd think.

Why does it happen?

  • Lifting something heavy suddenly, or lifting while twisting
  • Long sitting and weak back muscles from inactivity
  • Lack of sleep, stress (raising pain sensitivity)
  • A sudden jump in activity (moving house, big chores, holiday labor)
  • Obesity, smoking (reduced healing)

✅ Do you have these symptoms?

  • A dull, heavy ache in the middle or one side of the lower back
  • Pain in certain positions (long sitting, bending) that eases when you change position
  • Stiff in the morning, loosening as you move
  • Aching into the buttock at most, but no tingling below the knee
  • You can recall the day you hurt it or overdid it
  • No pain shooting into the leg with cough or sneeze

🔬 Self-observations you can do at home

Go slowly and stay within a pain-free range. These responses are observations, not a diagnosis.

🔍 Leg-symptom check (vs. a disc)

Check whether pain/tingling goes below the knee or to the toes. Lying down, slowly raise the affected straight leg.

For reference: If raising the leg only aches the back with no leg tingling, it leans toward non-specific back pain. If raising 30–70° sends electric pain below the knee, check the lumbar-disc page.

🔍 Directional preference test

Standing, slowly bend backward 10 times and forward 10 times, noting which direction reduces pain.

For reference: If a certain direction (often extension) reduces pain, that becomes your 'treatment movement.' If both directions hurt badly, don't push — see a doctor.

🔍 Red-flag screening

Check for fever, night pain, unexplained weight loss, bladder/bowel changes, or altered sensation in the groin.

For reference: If any apply, it may not be simple back pain — see a doctor before self-care.

🤲 Hands-on care — provided by a professional

🤲 Manual therapy — spinal mobilization & muscle release

The therapist gently moves stiff spinal joints and releases tense back and hip muscles. The role is to reduce acute pain so you can move sooner.

🤲 Heat / electrical therapy

A hot pack and electrical stimulation lower muscle tension and pain. Ice for the first 1–2 days of an acute strain, then heat is typical.

🤲 Medication (by a doctor)

When pain makes moving hard, anti-inflammatories or muscle relaxants create a 'body that can move.' Take the medicine to walk, not to go to bed.

🏃 Active care — exercises you do yourself

🏃 Walking (the best back treatment)

Start with a tolerable distance on flat ground and add a little each day. Walking nourishes the discs and wakes the muscles — the best free treatment.

⏱ 20–40 min a day (split into 10-minute bouts if needed)

🏃 McGill Big 3 ① Curl-up (abs)

Lie with one knee bent, hands under the lower back. Without flattening the back, lift just the head and shoulders slightly, holding 10 seconds.

⏱ 10 sec × 6 reps, once a day

🏃 McGill Big 3 ② Side plank (obliques)

Lie on your side, support on elbow and knee, and lift the hips into a straight line, holding 10 seconds. Progress to feet instead of knees.

⏱ 10 sec × 5 reps (each side), once a day

🏃 McGill Big 3 ③ Bird-dog (back & glutes)

On all fours, keep the back from wobbling and slowly reach one arm and the opposite leg out, holding 10 seconds. Keep the trunk so still a cup of water on your back wouldn't spill.

⏱ 10 sec × 5 reps (each side), once a day

🏃 Hip-hinge pattern (recurrence prevention)

With a stick on your back (keep 3 points — head, mid-back, tailbone — in contact), practice bowing by folding at the hips instead of the back. Using this pattern to lift and wash greatly reduces back load.

⏱ 10 reps × 2 sets, once a day

🚨 Stop self-care and see a doctor if…

  • Loss of bladder/bowel control or numb groin/perineum (cauda equina — go to the ER immediately)
  • Leg weakness so the foot drags or you can't lift toes/ankle
  • Fever with back pain (suspect infection)
  • New back pain with a history of cancer
  • Pain that doesn't ease at rest or at night and keeps worsening
  • Pain after major trauma (a fall, accident) or in an elderly person with osteoporosis
