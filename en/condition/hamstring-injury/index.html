Hamstring Injury (strain / tendinopathy)

Hamstring Strain / Tendinopathy

The back-thigh muscle suddenly tears (strain) or the attachment tendon wears (tendinopathy), making running and sitting painful.

✨ Open the interactive version (symptom check · compare)

📖 What is this condition?

The hamstrings are a group of three muscles on the back of the thigh that bend the knee and drive the leg backward. During a sprint, as the leg reaches forward, the hamstrings act as a brake while lengthening — and if more force than they can take hits in that instant, the muscle fibers tear with a 'pop.' It's the most common muscle injury in sports.

Another form is 'proximal hamstring tendinopathy,' where the tendon attaching to the sitting bone (the bone you sit on) slowly wears. Persistent pain at the sit-bone with long-distance running or long sitting is characteristic.

The biggest problem with hamstring injuries is recurrence. The re-injury rate is very high, mostly from rushing back with 'no pain = ready.' The muscle is still weak when pain disappears and isn't ready for speed.

Why does it happen?

  • Sprinting, sudden cutting or jumping (soccer, sprinting, baseball)
  • Sprinting while fatigued, with poor warm-up
  • Weak hamstrings, especially the 'lengthening-and-bracing' (eccentric) strength
  • A history of hamstring injury (the biggest risk factor)
  • Repeated stretching in long-distance running or yoga (tendinopathy type)

✅ Do you have these symptoms?

  • (Strain) A 'pop' and sudden pain in the back of the thigh while running
  • (Strain) A tender spot in the back of the thigh, sometimes with bruising
  • Pulling pain in the back of the thigh when raising the straight leg
  • Tight, painful back of the thigh with brisk walking or running
  • (Tendinopathy) Pain at the sit-bone when sitting
  • (Tendinopathy) Worse with uphill running or after stretching

🔬 Self-observations you can do at home

Go slowly and stay within a pain-free range. These responses are observations, not a diagnosis.

🔍 Estimating severity (strain)

Right after injury: check whether you can walk, how much knee-bend strength remains, and whether there's bruising or a divot.

For reference: Limping but walking suggests mild–moderate; difficulty walking, large bruising, or a felt divot suggests severe (partial–full tear) — see a doctor.

🔍 Straight-leg-raise angle comparison

Lie down and raise each straight leg, comparing the angle.

For reference: If the injured side rises noticeably less and the back of the thigh hurts, it suggests hamstring injury/shortening. Also good for tracking recovery.

🔍 Sit-bone tenderness + sitting pain (tendinopathy)

Press the sit-bone area and check pain when sitting on a hard chair a long time.

For reference: Sit-bone tenderness + sitting pain + running pain together suggest proximal hamstring tendinopathy.

🤲 Hands-on care — provided by a professional

🤲 Acute care (PEACE & LOVE)

The first few days: protect (limit hard walking), compress, elevate. Latest guidelines advise against 'excessive' anti-inflammatories and ice since they may slow healing — use briefly for pain only.

🤲 Manual therapy — soft-tissue work & pelvic alignment

In recovery, the therapist uses gentle massage and mobilization so the scar heals supple, and corrects pelvic/back movement issues to lower re-injury risk.

🤲 Shockwave (tendinopathy type)

Used for chronic proximal hamstring tendinopathy to stimulate healing of the worn tendon.

🏃 Active care — exercises you do yourself

🏃 Stage 1 — pain-free isometrics (a few days post-injury)

Lie down with the heel on the floor and press the heel down for 5–10 seconds. Giving the muscle an early 'go to work' signal within pain 3 speeds recovery.

⏱ 10 sec × 10 reps, twice a day

🏃 Stage 2 — hamstring bridge

Put the heels on a chair or sofa with knees slightly bent and lift the hips. Progress to single-leg as it gets easier.

⏱ 10 reps × 3 sets, once a day

🏃 Stage 3 — Romanian deadlift (RDL)

With light weight (or bodyweight), keep the back straight and send the hips back, lowering the torso so the hamstrings brace while lengthening. The 'work-while-lengthening' ability is the core of hamstring rehab.

⏱ 10 reps × 3 sets, 3 times a week

🏃 Stage 4 — Nordic curl (the king of recurrence prevention)

Kneel with ankles anchored and slowly topple forward in a straight line, bracing with the hamstrings. Proven in research to more than halve hamstring re-injury.

⏱ 5 reps × 2–3 sets, 2–3 times a week (high intensity — add after Stage 3)

🏃 Stage 5 — graded return to running

Brisk walk → easy jog → 70% speed → accel/decel drills → sprint, advancing only when there's no pain and no next-day reaction.

⏱ At least 3–7 days per stage

🚨 Stop self-care and see a doctor if…

  • A divot at the injury site or a feeling of a complete tear near the buttock (full tear/avulsion — a surgical candidate, see a doctor quickly)
  • Severe bruising and swelling making walking impossible
  • Tingling or electric feelings in the back of the thigh (rule out sciatic-nerve involvement)
  • In adolescents, severe sit-bone pain (rule out a growth-plate avulsion fracture)

⚠️ This material is for health information only and does not replace a doctor's diagnosis or treatment. If symptoms last more than 2 weeks or any red flag applies, see a medical professional.
