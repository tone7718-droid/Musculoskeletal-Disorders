Golfer's Elbow (Medial Epicondylitis)

The tendon of the wrist-flexing muscles weakens at the inner elbow bone, so gripping, pulling, and twisting hurt the inner elbow.

✨ Open the interactive version (symptom check · compare)

📖 What is this condition?

This is the 'inner version' of tennis elbow. The bony bump on the inside of the elbow is where the tendons of the wrist- and finger-flexing muscles attach. Repeated gripping, pulling, and twisting pile up micro-damage there, weakening and inflaming the tendon.

It's named for the impact of hitting fat (the ground) in a golf swing, but most patients have nothing to do with golf. Carrying heavy loads, hammering, rock climbing, baseball pitching, and repetitive hand work are common causes. It's less frequent than tennis elbow.

Why does it happen?

  • Repeated gripping, pulling, and twisting (carrying, tools, climbing)
  • Sports loading the wrist flexors (golf, baseball pitching)
  • A sudden increase in workload
  • Lack of wrist/shoulder flexibility and strength concentrating load on the elbow
  • Age 40s–50s tendon healing decline

✅ Do you have these symptoms?

  • Pain pressing the bony bump on the inner elbow
  • Pain in the inner elbow when gripping hard or lifting
  • Pain bending the wrist or turning inward (doorknob, bottle cap)
  • Pain radiating along the inner forearm toward the wrist
  • Morning stiffness in the elbow
  • (Sometimes) tingling on the ring/little-finger side too

🔬 Self-observations you can do at home

Go slowly and stay within a pain-free range. These responses are observations, not a diagnosis.

🔍 Resisted wrist flexion test

With the elbow straight and palm up, resist as someone presses your palm down while you flex the wrist.

For reference: Reproduced pain at the inner elbow bone is positive.

🔍 Golfer's elbow stretch test

Reach the affected arm forward, palm up, and with the other hand pull the fingers to bend the wrist back slowly.

For reference: Pulling and reproduced pain at the inner elbow is positive.

🔍 Nerve-symptom check

Note any tingling in the ring and little fingers, and tap the groove behind the inner elbow (the 'funny bone' spot).

For reference: If tapping sends an electric feeling to the little finger, ulnar nerve compression (cubital tunnel) may coexist — see a doctor.

🤲 Hands-on care — provided by a professional

🤲 Shockwave (ESWT)

Stimulates healing at the chronic tendon attachment. Like tennis elbow, the standard treatment for chronic golfer's elbow.

🤲 Manual therapy — inner-forearm flexor release & friction massage

The therapist releases the stiff inner forearm muscles and applies friction at the tendon attachment to reduce pain and aid healing.

🤲 Brace / taping

A strap below the inner elbow or taping spreads the tendon's pull to reduce pain during work.

🏃 Active care — exercises you do yourself

🏃 Isometric wrist flexion (pain-calming stage)

Support the forearm palm-up and resist with the other hand pressing the palm, without moving.

⏱ 30–45 sec × 5 reps, twice a day

🏃 Eccentric wrist flexion (core exercise)

Rest the forearm palm-up on a table holding a light dumbbell. Help lift it with the other hand, then lower it slowly over 3–4 seconds with the sore hand alone.

⏱ 15 reps × 3 sets, once a day

🏃 Forearm flexor stretch

Reach the arm out palm-up, and with the other hand pull the fingers to bend the wrist back gently, stretching the inner forearm.

⏱ 30 sec × 3 reps, 2–3 times a day

🏃 Pronation/supination (palm flips)

Elbow at your side bent 90°, hold one end of a hammer or water bottle and slowly flip the palm up and down. Builds a tendon strong in twisting.

⏱ 10 reps each direction × 2 sets, once a day

🏃 Shoulder & trunk strengthening (recurrence prevention)

Band rows and shoulder external rotation let the shoulder and trunk share the load. If you golf or pitch, checking your swing/throwing form is the best prevention.

⏱ 3 times a week

🚨 Stop self-care and see a doctor if…

  • Tingling in the ring/little fingers and wasting of hand muscles (ulnar nerve compression)
  • Swelling or limited bending/straightening of the elbow
  • Sudden onset after trauma with bruising (rule out tendon/ligament injury)
  • No improvement after 6+ months of treatment
