🦒 Neck

Forward Head Posture / Straight Neck

Your head sits in front of your body, so the neck and shoulder muscles spend all day holding up a heavy head.

✨ Open the interactive version (symptom check · compare)

📖 What is this condition?

A human head weighs about as much as a bowling ball (around 5 kg). When it sits directly over your shoulders, the bones carry that weight. But the more your head drifts forward — looking down at a phone or into a monitor — the more the back-of-neck muscles have to hold that bowling ball up like a rope. Every 2–3 cm the head moves forward adds several kilograms to what your neck feels.

The neck bones are naturally curved slightly forward in a 'C' (lordosis). When this posture lasts a long time, the curve flattens into a 'straight neck.' Because the curve acts like a shock-absorbing spring, a straight neck takes more of the same impact.

Why does it happen?

  • Looking down at or into a phone or monitor for long periods
  • Sleeping on a high pillow
  • A rounded upper back (a hunched back and forward head almost always come as a set)
  • Weak deep neck flexors while the back-of-neck muscles stay overly tense

✅ Do you have these symptoms?

  • By afternoon, the back of the neck and top of the shoulders feel heavy and stiff
  • In a side photo, your ear sits in front of your shoulder
  • Headaches that start at the back of the head and spread to the crown or eyes
  • Sitting a long time, the area between the shoulder blades aches before the neck does
  • Tucking the chin feels awkward and making a 'double chin' is hard
  • Tilting the head back feels stiff and the range is reduced

🔬 Self-observations you can do at home

Go slowly and stay within a pain-free range. These responses are observations, not a diagnosis.

🔍 Wall test

Stand with your back to a wall in your usual relaxed posture. With heels, buttocks, and upper back touching the wall, check whether the back of your head also touches.

For reference: If you have to force your neck back to touch the wall or feel pulling, that response can occur with a forward head position. This test alone cannot determine progression.

🔍 Ear–shoulder alignment

Take a side photo (or have someone look) and draw a vertical line through your ear canal and the middle of your shoulder (the acromion).

For reference: How far the ear appears in front of the shoulder can be a reference for observing alignment. Finger width alone cannot diagnose forward head posture.

🔍 Chin tuck test

Facing forward, pull your chin straight back to make a double chin and hold for 10 seconds.

For reference: Difficulty, shaking, or pulling can be a reason to observe neck-muscle endurance and movement control. This response alone does not confirm weakness.

🤲 Hands-on care — provided by a professional

🤲 Manual therapy — thoracic (upper back) mobilization

The hidden culprit behind forward head is a stiff upper back. When the therapist mobilizes the upper-back joints one by one, room is created for the head to return to place. Treating the upper back works better than treating only the neck.

🤲 Releasing the neck and shoulder muscles (massage, myofascial release)

The levator scapulae and upper trapezius, which hold the head up all day, are released by hand or tools. Pain and tightness ease, putting the body in a good state to exercise.

🤲 Heat and electrical therapy

A hot pack raises muscle temperature to improve circulation, and electrical stimulation reduces pain signals. Think of it as the 'start button' for treatment — it won't change your posture on its own.

🏃 Active care — exercises you do yourself

🏃 Chin tucks

Sit upright facing forward and pull your chin straight back to make a double chin. Feel the back of the neck lengthen, hold 5 seconds, then release slowly. The point is to push the whole head back, not to drop the head down.

⏱ 10 reps × 3 sets, 2–3 times a day (do a few any time, every hour)

🏃 Foam-roller upper-back extension

Lie with a foam roller across your upper back, below the shoulder blades. Support your head with both hands and arch the upper back over the roller. Keep the lower back from over-arching.

⏱ 10 reps × 2 sets, 1–2 times a day

🏃 Wall angels

Stand with your back against a wall, elbows bent 90° against the wall. Keeping your arms on the wall, slide them up and down slowly as if raising your arms overhead.

⏱ 10 reps × 3 sets, once a day

🏃 Levator scapulae / upper trapezius stretch

Grab the left side of your head with your right hand, look toward your right armpit, and pull the head diagonally. Keep the left shoulder pressed down so it doesn't ride up. Repeat on the other side.

⏱ 30 sec × 3 reps (each side), twice a day

🏃 Prone neck lifts (neck extensor strengthening)

Lie face down on a bed with your head just off the edge. Keeping the chin tucked, slowly lift your head only to the level of your trunk and hold 10 seconds.

⏱ 10-second holds × 10 reps, once a day

🚨 Stop self-care and see a doctor if…

  • Numbness or electric sensations spreading into the arm or hand (possible neck disc)
  • Weakness in the hand so you often drop things
  • Headache with dizziness, vision changes, or nausea
  • Pain severe enough to wake you at night that lasts more than 2 weeks
