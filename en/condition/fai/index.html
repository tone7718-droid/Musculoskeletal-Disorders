🦵 Hip · Thigh

Femoroacetabular Impingement (FAI)

The ball and socket of the hip don't fit smoothly, so bone pinches bone on deep bending and the groin hurts.

✨ Open the interactive version (symptom check · compare)

📖 What is this condition?

The hip is a ball-and-socket joint where the head of the thigh bone (ball) sits in the pelvic socket. If the ball or socket isn't smooth and part has grown extra (cam type: a bump on the ball; pincer type: an over-covering socket), the protrusion bumps the rim of cartilage (labrum) each time you bend deeply or rotate inward.

Picture a small bump on a door hinge — fine when opening a little, but it scrapes the frame at the end of the range. Repeated impingement tears the labrum and damages cartilage, causing hip pain at a young age and early arthritis. Common in athletes doing deep bending and rotation (soccer, hockey, ballet) and active adults aged 20–40.

Why does it happen?

  • Bone shape formed during growth (linked to heavy sport in the teen years)
  • Repeated deep squats, kicking, and cutting
  • Individual differences in socket depth and orientation
  • Weak hip muscles reaching the impingement angle more easily

✅ Do you have these symptoms?

  • Deep, heavy pain in the front of the groin (you cup it in a 'C' shape with your hand)
  • Pain squatting deeply, sitting cross-legged, or in low chairs
  • Stiff, sore groin standing up after long sitting
  • Discomfort getting in/out of a car or putting on socks
  • A pinching feeling, clicking or popping in the hip
  • Pain during/after exercise (squats, kicks, cutting)

🔬 Self-observations you can do at home

Go slowly and stay within a pain-free range. These responses are observations, not a diagnosis.

🔍 FADIR test (flexion-adduction-internal rotation)

Lie down, bend the affected hip and knee to 90°, bring the knee toward the opposite shoulder, and gently rotate the leg inward.

For reference: Reproduced familiar pain or pinching deep in the front groin suggests impingement/labral irritation.

🔍 Deep-squat test

Slowly squat as deep as you can with body weight.

For reference: If below a certain depth the groin pinches and hurts while shallow squats are fine, it suggests an impingement pattern.

🔍 The 'C sign'

Point to where it hurts.

For reference: If you cup the side of the hip/groin in a 'C' with thumb and index finger and describe it as 'deep inside,' that's the classic way people describe an intra-joint hip problem.

🤲 Hands-on care — provided by a professional

🤲 Manual therapy — hip mobilization & surrounding release

The therapist gently distracts and glides the thigh-bone head in the socket (long-axis traction, etc.) to make joint space, and releases the stiff hip flexors and adductors to reduce impingement load.

🤲 Injection (by a doctor)

An intra-articular injection calms inflammation and also helps confirm whether the pain source is inside the joint.

🤲 Activity modification coaching

You'll be guided to temporarily limit motions that hit the impingement angle (deep squats, sitting cross-legged, kicking). Reshaped bone can't be filed away by exercise, so 'avoiding the bumping angle while getting strong within it' is the core conservative strategy.

🏃 Active care — exercises you do yourself

🏃 Pain-free squats (box squat)

Sit and stand from a chair/box slightly higher than the depth that triggers pain. The goal is to keep and build leg strength within a pain-free range.

⏱ 10 reps × 3 sets, 3 times a week

🏃 Glute strengthening (bridge → single-leg bridge)

Start with glute bridges and progress to single-leg as you get used to it. Strong glutes move the hip into a better position within the socket.

⏱ 10–15 reps × 3 sets, once a day

🏃 Outer-hip strengthening (side plank + clamshell)

Strengthen the gluteus medius to improve pelvic stability when walking and running.

⏱ 10–15 reps × 3 sets each, once a day

🏃 Trunk-pelvis control (dead bug)

Lie with arms and legs up, and slowly lower the opposite arm/leg without letting the back arch. Pelvic-neutral control helps avoid the impingement angle.

⏱ 10 reps × 3 sets (each side), once a day

🏃 Hip 90/90 mobility

Sit on the floor with front and back legs bent 90°, sit tall, and tilt forward and back within a pain-free range. Restores mobility in non-impinging directions.

⏱ 10 reps × 2 sets (each side), once a day

🚨 Stop self-care and see a doctor if…

  • Sudden pain so severe you can't bear weight (rule out fracture/avascular necrosis)
  • Groin pain with fever
  • Pain that persists at night and rest
  • The hip feeling like it will dislocate or repeated locking
  • No improvement after 3–6 months of conservative care (surgery consult)
