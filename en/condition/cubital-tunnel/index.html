Cubital Tunnel Syndrome (ulnar nerve compression)

The ulnar nerve is squeezed in a groove on the inner elbow, so the ring and little fingers tingle and the hand weakens.

✨ Open the interactive version (symptom check · compare)

📖 What is this condition?

That nerve that zings to your little finger when you bump your inner elbow is the ulnar nerve. It passes through a shallow groove on the inside of the elbow (the cubital tunnel); being close to the surface and stretched tighter when the elbow bends, it's easily compressed. If carpal tunnel is 'median nerve / thumb side,' cubital tunnel is 'ulnar nerve / little-finger side.'

Sleeping with the elbow deeply bent (the phone posture), resting the elbow on a desk, and repeatedly pressing the inner elbow are common causes. Early on there's only ring/little-finger tingling; as it progresses, the hand's small muscles weaken so chopsticks, writing, and pinching get clumsy.

Caught early, correcting posture and habits recovers it well, but once muscles begin to waste, recovery is slow — so respond early.

Why does it happen?

  • Holding the elbow deeply bent for long (sleep, phone calls, reading)
  • Resting/pressing the inner elbow on a desk or armrest
  • Repeatedly bending and straightening the elbow at work/sport
  • A past elbow fracture or dislocation
  • An anatomical variant where the nerve slips out of the groove

✅ Do you have these symptoms?

  • Tingling and dullness in the ring (half) and little fingers
  • Tingling worse when the elbow is bent (phone, sleep)
  • Pressing or tapping the inner elbow sends an electric feeling to the little finger
  • Weak hand so you drop things often
  • Chopsticks, buttoning, and writing getting clumsy
  • (Advanced) the small muscles of the hand look wasted

🔬 Self-observations you can do at home

Go slowly and stay within a pain-free range. These responses are observations, not a diagnosis.

🔍 Elbow-flexion hold test

Hold the elbow fully bent with the wrist straight for 3 minutes.

For reference: Reproduced or worsened ring/little-finger tingling suggests ulnar nerve compression.

🔍 Tinel's sign (elbow groove)

Lightly tap the groove behind the bony bump on the inner elbow.

For reference: An electric feeling shooting to the little finger when tapped is positive.

🔍 Hand strength/muscle check

Pinch paper between thumb and index and resist, and spread/close the fingers, comparing both hands.

For reference: If the affected side is weak or the muscle between thumb and index is wasted, nerve damage may be progressing — see a doctor.

🤲 Hands-on care — provided by a professional

🤲 Night splint (keeping the elbow straighter)

A splint/brace holding the elbow slightly straight at night so it doesn't bend deeply greatly reduces night tingling. A core early treatment.

🤲 Manual therapy — neural mobilization & release

The therapist glides the ulnar nerve through the groove smoothly and releases tense forearm flexors to reduce compression.

🤲 Surgical consult (if progressing)

If muscle wasting begins or conservative care fails, surgery to relieve compression (widening the tunnel or moving the nerve forward) is considered.

🏃 Active care — exercises you do yourself

🏃 Posture/habit correction (most important)

Use earphones for calls, prevent deep elbow-bending in sleep (loosely wrap a towel in front of the elbow), and don't rest the elbow on a desk. Cutting the cause is half the treatment.

⏱ Lifestyle — always

🏃 Ulnar nerve gliding

Reach the arm to the side palm-up, make an 'OK' with the fingers, and bring it toward the eye like glasses and back. Glides the nerve gently. Reduce range if tingling worsens.

⏱ 10 reps × 2 sets, twice a day

🏃 Forearm flexor stretch

Reach the arm out palm-up and bend the fingers down to stretch the inner forearm.

⏱ 30 sec × 3 reps, twice a day

🏃 Hand intrinsic exercise (after symptoms ease)

Wake the weakened hand muscles by holding a card pinched between fingers and spreading the fingers against a rubber band.

⏱ 10 reps × 2 sets, once a day

🚨 Stop self-care and see a doctor if…

  • The muscle between thumb and index is wasted compared to the other side (muscle wasting — see a doctor quickly)
  • Clearly weak finger spreading/closing strength
  • Tingling all day that doesn't ease with position changes
  • Onset after trauma with elbow deformity or instability
  • Worsening despite several months of conservative care
