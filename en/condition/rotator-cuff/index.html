🏋️ Shoulder

Rotator Cuff Tendinopathy / Tear

The four tendons that wrap and stabilize the shoulder wear down or tear, so raising and rotating the arm hurts and feels weak.

✨ Open the interactive version (symptom check · compare)

📖 What is this condition?

The rotator cuff is a set of four muscle tendons (supraspinatus, infraspinatus, teres minor, subscapularis) wrapping the shoulder. Like a ball balanced on a cup, the unstable shoulder joint is held centered by these tendons from all sides.

Long use that wears the tendon down is 'tendinopathy'; partial or full tearing is a 'tear.' Picture a rope fraying and finally unraveling. After age 50, partial tears from wear are common even without injury — and remarkably, many people live well, pain-free, even with a tear.

So the key is 'is the shoulder still working?' more than 'is it torn?' Many partial tears recover good function without surgery through exercise therapy.

Why does it happen?

  • Natural age-related tendon degeneration (rising sharply after 40–50)
  • Long-standing impingement fraying the tendon
  • Trauma — falling onto an outstretched arm, suddenly lifting something heavy
  • Jobs/sports with lots of overhead motion (papering, baseball, badminton, swimming)
  • Smoking, diabetes (reduced blood flow and healing capacity)

✅ Do you have these symptoms?

  • Deep shoulder pain when raising or rotating the arm
  • Night pain that wakes you, especially lying on the affected side
  • A feeling of weakness or the arm 'dropping' when raised
  • Pain spreading from the outer shoulder toward the elbow
  • Difficulty combing hair, fastening a bra, reaching the back seat
  • Lowering the arm slowly isn't controlled — it drops suddenly

🔬 Self-observations you can do at home

Go slowly and stay within a pain-free range. These responses are observations, not a diagnosis.

🔍 Empty-can test (supraspinatus)

Raise both arms to shoulder height, angle about 30° forward, and turn the thumbs down (as if emptying a can). Resist as someone presses your wrists down.

For reference: If only the affected side hurts or gives way weakly, suspect a supraspinatus tendon problem.

🔍 External-rotation strength comparison

Elbow at your side bent 90°, resist outward as someone pushes your wrist inward. Compare both sides.

For reference: If the affected side is clearly weak with pain, suspect infraspinatus involvement.

🔍 Drop-arm test

Raise the affected arm fully out to the side (help it up with the other hand if needed), then lower it very slowly to the side.

For reference: If the arm drops uncontrolled partway down, a large tear is possible — see a doctor promptly.

🤲 Hands-on care — provided by a professional

🤲 Manual therapy — joint mobilization & soft-tissue release

The therapist restores joint glide and releases the stiff posterior capsule and chest muscles to create a good working environment for the tendon. The aim isn't to 'glue' the tendon but to reduce its load.

🤲 Shockwave (ESWT)

Gives the worn tendon micro-stimulation to wake blood flow and healing. Especially used for chronic tendinopathy and when calcium deposits are present.

🤲 Injection / medication (by a doctor)

During an inflammatory phase, an anti-inflammatory injection can lower pain so exercise can start. Repeated steroid injections can weaken the tendon, so they are limited.

🏃 Active care — exercises you do yourself

🏃 Pendulum exercise (acute starter)

Lean on a table, let the affected arm hang, and gently sway your trunk so the arm swings like a pendulum. With the shoulder fully relaxed, it nourishes the joint.

⏱ 1–2 minutes, 3–4 times a day

🏃 Isometric external rotation (calming tendon pain)

Stand beside a doorframe with the back of your hand against it; push outward for 5 seconds without moving the arm (intensity at pain 3 or under). A safe starting stage shown to reduce tendon pain.

⏱ 5 sec × 10 reps × 2 sets, twice a day

🏃 Band external/internal rotation

Elbow at your side, pull the band slowly outward/inward. The 'slow return (3 seconds)' is the key to tendon recovery — don't let it snap back.

⏱ 15 reps each direction × 3 sets, once a day

🏃 Side-lying external rotation (0.5–1 kg dumbbell)

Lie on the unaffected side, the affected elbow at your side bent 90°, and slowly raise and lower a light dumbbell.

⏱ 12 reps × 3 sets, once a day

🏃 Scapular stabilization alongside

Add wall push-up plus, Y-T-W, etc. A stable launch pad (the blade) reduces the cuff's load.

⏱ 10 reps each × 2 sets, once a day

🚨 Stop self-care and see a doctor if…

  • Can't raise the arm at all right after trauma (acute large tear — see a doctor quickly, especially when young)
  • The arm drops uncontrolled in the drop-arm test
  • The shoulder muscle (upper back of the shoulder) visibly wastes away
  • No change in pain or function after 3+ months of diligent rehab
  • Night pain worsening and painkillers no longer helping

⚠️ This material is for health information only and does not replace a doctor's diagnosis or treatment. If symptoms last more than 2 weeks or any red flag applies, see a medical professional.
